---
id: mapPinCards
title: What do the pins and cards on the map mean?
category: map
minAppVersion: "1.45.0"
---

Each contact on the map is a round pin with their initials, colored by how long it's been since your last visit (tap the ⓘ button on the map to see what each color means, or change the colors in Settings → Preferences → Personalization → Color Key). A small mark on the pin's corner shows a follow-up that's overdue (red clock), a follow-up today (green clock), or a favorite (star).

Beside a pin, a card shows the contact's name and one detail. By default the detail is their next visit: an overdue follow-up, an upcoming follow-up with its day and time, or otherwise when you last visited. Cards for overdue and upcoming follow-ups are placed first, and cards that would cover another pin or card wait until you zoom in, so zoom in to see more. The selected contact's card turns green.

To choose what cards show, go to Settings → Preferences → Map, or tap the layers button on the map and choose Pin cards show. You can pick Next visit, Last visit, Follow-up topic, Name only, or any of your contact fields, such as a Language field when you work a language territory. A card shows only the name when that contact has no topic or no value in the field.

When pins sit on top of each other, they gather into one square pin with a count. Tap it: contacts at the same address, such as an apartment building, open in a list with their apartment or unit, most urgent first, and tapping a name selects that contact. Contacts that are only close together zoom the map in so their pins spread out.

The chip at the top of the map counts the contacts in view who are overdue or due in the next seven days. Tap it to keep only them bright, with their cards, and tap it again to see everyone. When today has a route, the Today's Route chip opens it. To plan a route from contacts on the map, see "How do I plan a route from the map?".

Pins and cards are worked out on your device and nothing is sent anywhere to draw them. The map doesn't rotate or tilt, so the cards stay next to their pins. This works the same on iPhone, iPad, and Android, and it's free. If a contact is missing from the map, see "Why are some contacts missing from the map?". If a card shows the wrong detail, check the contact's follow-up or field value; for other problems, use the Help Center's private support option and keep names and addresses out of public reports.
