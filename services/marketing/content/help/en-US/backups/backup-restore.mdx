For an automatic, end-to-end encrypted backup, turn on Backup in Settings → Account; see "What is WitnessWork Backup?". For a backup file, open Settings → Backup & Restore → Create Backup or Restore From Backup to export or restore a JSON backup on iOS or Android. From version 1.46, Create Backup locks the file, and you choose how. My Account locks it to your account: it restores on your devices signed in to your account, or anywhere after you enter your recovery key. Password locks it with a password of at least 8 characters: it restores on any device with that password, which is how to share a copy. Without the password or your account, nobody can read a locked file, and WitnessWork can't recover a forgotten password. Locking or opening a file with a password takes a few seconds. Versions before 1.46 can't restore a locked file, so update WitnessWork on the other device first; files made before 1.46 aren't locked and still restore. To restore, choose the witness-work-backup.json file you saved with Create Backup, and enter its password if it has one. If WitnessWork says to sign in first, sign in to your account on this device with Sign In with QR Code or your recovery key, then restore again. If it says the file is locked to another account, restore it on a device signed in to that account, or create a password-locked backup there. On a new device, you can also choose From a backup file on the “Pick up where you left off” setup screen; restoring there finishes setup with your restored profile. Backups include service records, plans, contacts, visits, categories, mileage, your pet, profile, and shared preferences. A restore replaces the included local data; older backups may omit categories or profile, which are kept as they are, and contacts in older backups keep their custom field values. A backup made with a newer version of WitnessWork can't be restored until you update WitnessWork on this device. If some items in a backup can't be read, the rest is restored and WitnessWork tells you how many items were left out. Device identity, sync settings, data protection mode, analytics consent, and onboarding progress stay local to this device. Photo files are not embedded in JSON backups. For iCloud or Google Drive photos, turn on Include images in sync separately; from version 1.47.0, Cloud Sync carries photos on its own (see “Do photos sync with Cloud Sync?”). A backup restores on iPhone or Android, so it can move your records from one to the other; to keep both in step instead, use Cloud Sync (see “What is Cloud Sync?”). Save a current backup before restoring, and keep the file and its password private; it can contain householder details. Use the Help Center’s private support option for recovery help.
